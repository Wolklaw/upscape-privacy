Upscape Privacy Policy

Effective October 11, 2026

Upscape is a single-player arcade game made by Wolklaw. This policy covers the Upscape app for Android, Windows (including the Steam version) and the web, including the free web demo.

What we collect

Nothing. Upscape does not collect, use, sell or share any personal information or device data. The game has no ads, ever, no accounts, no analytics and no tracking, and it does not need an internet connection to play.

Google Play and Steam versions

The versions of Upscape on Google Play and Steam make no network requests of their own. The Android app from Google Play does not have the internet permission. Updates come from the store.

Supporter Pack. On Google Play, the optional one-dollar Supporter Pack (an interface theme and a skin) is bought through Google Play's billing system. Google Play handles the payment and your payment details under its own terms and privacy policy; the game never sees them. The game only learns from Google Play whether your account owns the pack, so it can unlock it.

Leaderboards. Global leaderboards are off by default. Only if you turn on GLOBAL LEADERBOARDS in the game's Settings (Google Play version), and you are signed in to Play Games, the game sends your scores to Google Play Games, which shows them under your Play Games profile and gamer name. Google Play Games is operated by Google under its own privacy policy, and you can manage or delete your Play Games profile and data at any time from your Play Games settings. If you leave the setting off, nothing is sent and Play Games is never opened.

Redeem codes

Friends and family of the developer may receive a code that unlocks the paid extras. The game checks the code on your device. To make your personal code, the developer needs your player ID: a random number the game generates on your device, shown on the Redeem screen. It is not linked to your name, your account or your device, and the game never sends it anywhere; you choose whether to share it. A Google Play promo code entered in the game is opened in the Play Store, which redeems it under Google's terms.

Direct downloads

A copy of Upscape that you get directly from the developer, rather than from Google Play or Steam, has two extra features. The Support screen has a tip button that opens paypal.me/wolklaw in your browser. Nothing is sent to PayPal by the game, and tipping is handled entirely by PayPal under its own terms and privacy policy. And when your device is online, the game asks GitHub, where the developer publishes direct downloads, whether a newer version exists, so it can offer to update. This request contains no personal information; like any web request, GitHub can see your IP address. On Android, the update is only downloaded when you tap the update button; on Windows, the update button opens the download page in your browser.

Web demo

The free demo at wolklaw.github.io/upscape-demo runs entirely in your browser and makes no network requests of its own: no tip button, no update check, no analytics. Its scores and settings stay in your browser's storage for that site, and clearing your browser data deletes them. The page is hosted on GitHub Pages, so, like any website, GitHub receives your IP address when you load it, under GitHub's own privacy statement.

What stays on your device

The game saves your high scores, the name you type for the high score board, which climbers and extras you have unlocked, your player ID, any redeem codes you entered and your settings, such as sound and level width. This information is stored only on your device (on Windows, in the Upscape folder inside your local app data) and is never sent to us or anyone else. Uninstalling the app deletes it.

On Android, if you use your device's backup feature, the backup may include this saved game data, the same as for any other app. That backup is handled by your device and your Google account, not by us.

Permissions

The Android app from Google Play asks for no dangerous permissions; to sell the Supporter Pack it declares Google Play's billing permission, which only lets it talk to the Play Store. A directly downloaded Android app uses internet access only to check for updates, and asks for no other permissions.

Children

The game itself collects no information from anyone, including children. Purchases and Play Games sign-in go through Google Play, which applies its own protections for children's accounts, such as parental approval of purchases.

Changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted on this page with a new effective date.
